Label-free RL for reasoning

Co-RL: Unsupervised Reasoning Emerges from Diverse Cohort in Multi-agent RL

Self-rewarding RL reinforces whatever the model already believes. How different does a peer have to be before its vote is worth learning from?

01 / The problem

Labels made RL for reasoning work. Labels are also its bottleneck.

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards is the standard recipe for stronger reasoning, and it needs a checked answer for every training question. Those answers are costly to produce, and they become scarce exactly where they matter most: as target capabilities approach what humans can reliably evaluate.

01 · THE LABEL BILL Someone has to write the answer key

Every training question needs a verified answer. Those answers are costly to produce, and the frontier questions are exactly the ones humans can no longer check reliably.

02 · THE SHORTCUT Self-rewarding drops the labels and lets the model grade itself

The reward comes from the model's own behavior: its majority vote (TTRL), its confidence (Intuitor), its entropy (RENT), or agreement with a copy of itself (Co-rewarding).

03 · THE CATCH A model that grades itself is rewarded for confidence, not correctness

Whatever it already believes gets reinforced. Prolonged training can homogenize responses, degenerate, or collapse. Section 05 shows the training curves where this happens.

02 / The idea

Co-RL takes the reward from a peer, never from the model itself.

If the failure comes from the model grading itself, the fix is structural: keep the majority vote, but take it from someone else. Multiple models with separate parameters train side by side, and each one is rewarded when its answer matches a peer's vote. The self-confirmation loop cannot close, because the target comes from independently updated weights.

STEP 01 Every agent samples and votes

Each of N agents independently samples K completions for the same unlabeled prompt and reduces them to a self-consistency majority vote.

STEP 02 The peer's vote becomes the reward

An agent earns reward 1 when its answer matches the vote of the previous agent on the ring, and 0 otherwise. With two agents, they simply grade each other. An agent never contributes to its own supervision target.

STEP 03 Each policy updates alone

Rewards are normalized within each agent's own rollout group, and each policy takes its own GRPO step. No shared parameters, no exchanged gradients. The one exchanged vote per prompt is the entire coupling.

Self-rewarding · TTRL, Co-rewarding

The reward is computed from the model's own agreement with itself: its majority vote, a paraphrase, or a moving-average copy of the same weights.

Judge-based multi-agent · CoMAS

Agents debate in multi-turn interactions, and one of the system's own agents acts as an LLM judge that scores the exchange into a reward.

Co-RL

No judge, no learned reward model, no message passing. One exchanged vote per prompt, from an independently trained peer, along a directed ring.

04 / Why it works

More different partners make better teachers.

Separate parameters stop the degeneration, but they do not decide how much a peer can teach. A partner that fails on the same problems hands an agent back its own error. So the question behind the gains is concrete: do two models make the same mistakes? That is measurable before any training happens.

Score two base checkpoints on the same 500 MATH problems (levels 3 to 5, zero-shot, single sample at temperature 0.8, rule-based grading). Every problem lands in one of four cells:

B correctB wrong
A correctboth correctonly A correct
A wrongonly B correctboth wrong

Four numbers summarize a pair, and every one of them is a count over these four cells:

κError overlap. How often the two models' right-and-wrong patterns coincide, beyond chance. 1 means they always fail together, 0 means their errors are unrelated. Lower is better for co-training.
cComplementarity. The share of problems where exactly one model is correct. This is the territory where a peer can correct a mistake. Higher is better.
wWrong agreement. The share where both models are wrong with the same answer. A majority vote cannot catch this case. Lower is better.
uAt least one right. The share of problems outside the both-wrong cell. Higher means more problems are recoverable by some member of the pair.
The finding Every different-family pair reaches κ ≤ 0.42 and c ≥ 29.4%. Every same-family and seed-only pair sits at κ ≥ 0.51 and c ≤ 24.6%. No pair falls between the two groups, at either tier. Averaged within groups, crossing families lowers κ from 0.53 to 0.38 and raises c from 23.2% to 30.8%. Holding one model fixed and varying only its partner gives the same ordering, so capability is not the explanation. And same-family pairs are not distinguishable from seed-only pairs: changing the size, the generation, or the sampling seed within a lineage leaves the error structure where it was.

06 / Objections

The three obvious objections have controlled answers.

You trained two models. You ensembled at test time. Multi-agent RL already does this. Each objection gets an experiment with the budget matched, and the numbers stay next to their protocol.

07 / Theory

A peer's vote can rescue a prompt you would get wrong alone.

The empirical sections show that diversity works. The theory section of the paper says when, by reducing each prompt to two outcomes and tracking the chance that an agent answers it correctly under GRPO updates.

Self-rewarding is self-confirming. When the pseudo-label comes from the same rollouts being optimized, the expected update pushes toward whichever answer is currently more likely, whether it is correct or not. A prompt the model starts out wrong on sinks to always wrong. Self-rewarding cannot recover it (Proposition 2 in the paper).

Co-RL enlarges the set of prompts that end up right. With the reward taken from a peer, the update direction of one agent is set entirely by its partner's vote. The paper shows the two-agent system converges to the correct answer whenever the two agents' chances of being right sum to more than 1 (Theorem 1). That is a strictly weaker requirement than each agent individually being right more often than not, which is what self-rewarding needs.

Takeaway: complementary strengths get exploited. In the paper's worked example, one agent is right 90% of the time on half the prompts and 20% on the other half, and its partner is the mirror image. Each averages 55%. Self-rewarding converges to 50% accuracy. Co-RL converges to 100%, because on every prompt the two chances sum to 1.1.
Where it stops Wrong-together is also stable: a pair that starts below the line converges together on the wrong answer. Co-RL widens the good basin, it does not remove the bad one. That failure mode is exactly what wrong-agreement w measures in section 04, and it is lowest for different-family pairs (1.8% for Qwen2.5-7B with Llama-3.1-8B) and highest for seed-only ones (5.2%). How the number, diversity, and interaction topology of agents shape cross-agent learning remains open. The directed ring is one topology among several.

08 / Run it

Every setting on this page is one launcher away.

The public release is Apache-2.0 and ships the baselines alongside the method. The rungs of section 04 are literally the launcher filenames.

Repository map

  • llm/ holds the text experiments on MATH levels 3 to 5: trainers/co_rl/ (Co-RL), trainers/self_rewarding/ (TTRL, RENT, Intuitor), trainers/grpo/ (the GT-Reward reference), and eval/ (the seven-benchmark suite).
  • mllm/ holds the vision-language experiments on open-r1 and MMR1, with the four-benchmark evaluation suite.
  • tools/verify.py asserts the installed dependency versions match the paper runs.

The configuration behind the reported numbers

Optimizer
AdamW; rollout temperature 1.0
Text runs
lr 3e-6, effective batch 128 prompts per agent, 3072-token cap, K = 12, 2 epochs
Vision-language runs
lr 1e-6, 1024-token cap, K = 8, 1 epoch. Gemma-3 runs add token-level truncation of the importance-sampling ratio (measured log-probability drift of about 0.13)
Hardware
One node of eight H100 GPUs, four per agent
Text eval
T = 0.6, top-p 0.95. AMC is avg@8 over three seeds. GPQA uses the Diamond subset with a boxed prompt. LiveCodeBench v6 runs its official harness
Vision-language eval
Greedy decoding, 16384-token budget, images resized to a 1024 px long side. Two-stage grading: the LLM judge only recovers rule-grading false negatives and never overturns a rule-credited answer
